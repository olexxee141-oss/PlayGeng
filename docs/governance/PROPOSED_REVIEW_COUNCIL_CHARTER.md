# Community Review Council Charter — Ratified November 11, 2026

**Status:** Ratified by the user on November 11, 2026. This document is the approved Review Council charter and implementation contract. Future amendments require explicit Community Assembly approval.

This charter completes the Judiciary design decisions in [Three Arms of Community Governance](../governance/THREE_ARMS_COMMUNITY_GOVERNANCE.md). It preserves the Assembly's final authority while giving the Judiciary enough independence and process to review constitutional and procedural disputes.

## 1. Composition and independence

**Ratified structure: three elected Review Council members, plus two elected alternates.**

- The three members form the standing Review Council.
- The two alternates are elected in the same process and in ranked order. They have no voting or case-decision authority unless formally substituted for a recused, unavailable, or removed Council member.
- No person may simultaneously hold an Executive office and a Council seat or alternate seat.
- The creator has no appointment, removal, or veto power over Council membership.
- Council members and alternates receive no general Executive permissions and no Treasury write permissions.
- A Council member's role is constitutional review, procedural adjudication, and conflict determination within the Judiciary's defined jurisdiction—not routine administration.

Why this structure: three active members keep the body small; two elected alternates provide a defined replacement path when conflicts or vacancies would otherwise destroy quorum.

## 2. Election, eligibility, and term

- Council members and alternates are elected directly by the eligible Community Assembly, separately from Executive elections.
- Candidates must be active community members in good standing and must not hold an Executive office while serving.
- A candidate must disclose material conflicts and any current disciplinary restriction relevant to impartial service before the election.
- Use the existing one-member/one-vote election foundation. Each seat is filled only by a candidate who satisfies the community's ratified election quorum and majority rule. Unfilled seats remain vacant; do not appoint a runner-up who failed the threshold.
- **Ratified term: 12 months**, with a maximum of two consecutive full terms.
- The initial three seats should be staggered only if the election system can do so transparently without arbitrary term assignment; otherwise, use simultaneous first terms and adopt staggered renewal at the next constitutional review.
- A Council member may resign. A vacancy is filled by the highest-ranked eligible alternate who accepts and remains conflict-free; if no alternate is eligible, hold a by-election.
- Removal before term end requires a recall vote of the Assembly under the same notice, frozen-electorate, quorum, and majority safeguards used for other governance decisions. A Council member cannot adjudicate their own recall.

## 3. Case assignment and recusals

- The Council acts as a body; no single reviewer may issue a binding Judiciary decision alone.
- All members must disclose direct, material personal or financial conflicts before assignment.
- A reviewer must recuse from a matter involving their own obligation or request, a close personal or financial interest, a complaint against their own conduct, or a decision they personally made in another capacity.
- The next eligible alternate replaces a recused member for that case. The substitution and reason category are recorded in the restricted case audit trail.
- The requester, subject of a complaint, or conflicted officer cannot select, remove, or replace reviewers.
- If all alternates are conflicted or unavailable, the case is paused. The Assembly must elect a temporary conflict-free reviewer through a case-specific, publicly auditable election; neither the Executive nor the subject of the case may appoint one.
- A reviewer may be challenged for conflict. The challenged reviewer does not decide their own challenge; the remaining conflict-free Council members decide it, with an alternate substituted if necessary.
- Recusal details remain private. The parties receive the reason category and enough information to challenge impartiality; the wider Assembly receives only the procedural record necessary for accountability.

## 4. Quorum and decision threshold

- **Quorum: two of the three assigned, conflict-free reviewers.**
- **Decision: at least two affirmative votes are required** to issue a binding ruling.
- If only two reviewers are eligible, both must agree. If they disagree, no binding ruling is issued; the matter is referred to the Assembly for a new, conflict-free panel election or other remedy expressly authorized by the constitution.
- A tie, failed quorum, or inability to obtain two affirmative votes is not a ruling on the merits. The status remains unresolved and must not silently validate the challenged action.
- Decisions must state jurisdiction, applicable constitutional rule, findings, remedy, vote, recusals, and effective date.
- A reviewer may append a dissenting opinion. Dissent does not delay an otherwise valid decision.

## 5. Jurisdiction and remedies

The Council may:

- Interpret the community constitution and Assembly-approved policy.
- Determine whether an Executive officer exceeded authority or failed to implement a final Assembly decision.
- Decide procedural challenges concerning notice, electorate eligibility, conflicts, quorum, voting integrity, privacy, and required audit records.
- Determine disputed recusals for Assembly case votes, subject to the recusal rules above.
- Review whether an Assembly decision complied with the constitution and ratified procedure.

The Council may not:

- Decide whether a member deserves an exemption or waiver; that merits decision belongs to the Assembly.
- Rewrite policy because it disagrees with the Assembly's policy choice.
- Create or amend financial obligations, edit posted journal entries, approve withdrawals, or execute Treasury adjustments.
- Appoint or remove Executive officers outside the Assembly's ratified election/recall process.

Available remedies should be limited to:
1. Dismiss a challenge that is outside jurisdiction or unsupported.
2. Declare a procedural or constitutional violation.
3. Order the responsible arm to correct a remediable process defect.
4. Suspend the effect of a challenged decision only where necessary to prevent irreversible harm and where the constitution expressly authorizes interim relief.
5. Remit a procedurally invalid Assembly vote for a fresh vote under the ratified rules.

The Council must not replace a substantive Assembly decision with its own preferred outcome.

## 6. Appeals and challenge periods

- **Recommended filing period: seven calendar days** after the affected person receives private notice of the decision, or after the decision is publicly recorded where the challenger is not a direct party.
- A challenge must identify the constitutional or procedural ground and the remedy requested.
- Filing a challenge does not automatically stay the underlying decision.
- The Council may grant a temporary stay only under the urgent-relief rule below.
- The Council's decision is final within the Judiciary for that case. A further appeal lies to the Assembly only on a constitutional interpretation question or a material procedural defect in the Council's own process; the Assembly may remit the case but may not use an appeal to decide a member's private hardship merits without the required case vote.
- A second challenge on the same facts and grounds is barred unless material new evidence or a continuing procedural defect is demonstrated.

## 7. Urgent temporary relief

**Ratified rule: narrow interim relief is permitted, but not for the underlying merits of an exemption request.**

- A party may request interim relief where immediate action risks irreversible harm or would make the eventual review meaningless.
- At least two conflict-free Council members must approve it.
- Relief must be narrowly tailored, time-limited, reasoned, and logged.
- Interim relief may preserve the status quo, pause a disputed governance action, or prevent execution of a contested non-routine decision.
- It must not authorize a transfer, change a ledger balance, erase an obligation, or grant an exemption/waiver.
- A stay affecting an Assembly decision expires after 7 calendar days unless the Council issues a reasoned extension or the Assembly takes the matter up under its own authority.
- If a valid Council quorum cannot be formed, no individual reviewer or Executive officer may grant interim relief.

## 8. Transitional rule before the Council is elected

Until the Review Council is elected and operational:

- Do not implement Assembly exemption/waiver voting in production if a disputed recusal could arise without an independent decision-maker.
- Do not allow the requester, subject, conflicted officer, creator, or ordinary Executive officer to decide a disputed recusal.
- A disputed case remains pending; the underlying obligation and ledger remain unchanged.
- The Assembly may first elect the Review Council under a one-time, published election procedure. No temporary Judiciary power is granted to the Executive.
- Existing ratified case-vote rules remain binding; this transitional rule does not amend them.

## 9. Ratified decisions

The following decisions are ratified and must be implemented as written:

1. Three Council members plus two alternates.
2. Independent Assembly election and no concurrent Executive office.
3. Active-member eligibility and disclosure requirements.
4. Twelve-month term and two-consecutive-term limit.
5. Vacancy and alternate substitution process.
6. Assembly recall process.
7. Two-of-three quorum and two affirmative votes for a binding decision.
8. Defined jurisdiction and limited remedies.
9. Seven-calendar-day challenge period and appeal boundaries.
10. Narrow, time-limited interim relief.
11. Transitional rule: disputed cases remain pending until an independent Council is operational.
